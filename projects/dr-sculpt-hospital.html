Centralized HVAC & OT Solutions for Dr. Sculpt Hospital

Healthcare Project

Project overview

Clean-air operating theatres and centralized comfort cooling engineered to healthcare hygiene and air-change standards.

The engagement followed our full HVAC lifecycle, a detailed site assessment and heat-load study, right-sized system selection, clean installation and commissioning, followed by testing, balancing and a structured maintenance handover. The outcome is dependable comfort with efficient, predictable operation.

The brief

Dr. Sculpt Hospital needed HVAC that could meet two very different demands under a single roof, comfort-grade centralized cooling for patient areas, waiting rooms and consulting spaces, and clinical-grade, clean-air conditioning for its operating theatres. The brief was clear that OT air handling could not be treated as an extension of the general comfort system; it needed its own filtration, pressure control and air-change discipline aligned with healthcare hygiene practice, while the rest of the facility needed dependable, even comfort cooling for patients and staff throughout the day. Getting this balance right meant treating the OT and the general hospital as two separate design problems from the outset, rather than scaling one solution across both. The hospital administration was also clear that any solution had to be serviceable without repeated disruption to patient care.

The challenge

Operating theatres and clinical areas are unforgiving of disruption, cooling and air handling had to be engineered and installed without compromising infection-control protocols or interrupting the hospital's working schedule. Maintaining positive pressure in the OT relative to surrounding areas, controlling particulate ingress and preventing cross-contamination between clinical and general zones added a layer of precision beyond a typical commercial installation. Coordinating civil, electrical and medical-gas contractors on an active hospital site meant every stage of work needed careful sequencing and constant communication with hospital administration. Noise and vibration from equipment placement also had to be considered carefully, given the proximity of patient recovery areas and consulting rooms to the plant and ductwork.

Our engineered solution

We designed a centralized system for general hospital areas to deliver consistent comfort cooling with minimal noise and draught, paired with a dedicated, higher-specification air-handling approach for the OT suite, built around filtration, controlled air changes and pressure differentials appropriate to a clinical environment. Ducting and diffuser layouts were planned to avoid turbulence near sterile fields, and the OT zone was kept mechanically independent of the general system so that servicing one area never affects the other. Materials and finishes around the clinical air-handling equipment were also chosen with cleanability and infection-control practice in mind. This separation also means the OT can continue operating uninterrupted during routine maintenance or servicing work elsewhere in the hospital.

Coordinated multi-trade installation

Civil, electrical and medical-gas contractors were all working on the active hospital site alongside our team, so every stage of ductwork, wiring and equipment placement was sequenced and communicated with hospital administration in advance.

Execution & commissioning

Work was sequenced around the hospital's live schedule, with OT-area installation and testing carried out in close coordination with clinical staff to avoid any impact on procedures. Every stage, ductwork, insulation, filtration and controls, was tested and validated before handover, and the OT air-handling setup was commissioned with particular attention to airflow direction, pressure balance and noise levels appropriate to a clinical setting. Handover included clear documentation for the hospital's facility team, covering routine checks specific to the OT air-handling system.

The outcome

Dr. Sculpt Hospital now operates with a clear separation between clinical-grade OT conditioning and general-comfort cooling, each engineered to its own standard rather than compromised by the other. Patients and staff experience consistent, quiet comfort through the wards and consulting areas, while the OT suite runs on air handling built specifically for hygiene and precision. A structured maintenance plan keeps both systems performing reliably as patient volumes grow, with scheduled checks designed around the stricter reliability expectations of a clinical environment.

Outcome. The OT suite now runs on clean-air handling engineered specifically for hygiene and precision, fully independent of the hospital's general comfort cooling.
