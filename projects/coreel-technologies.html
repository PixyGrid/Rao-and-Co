HVAC Solutions for CoreEL Technologies

Industrial Project

Project overview

End-to-end HVAC design and installation for CoreEL's electronics manufacturing and R&D facility, balancing precision cooling with energy efficiency.

The engagement followed our full HVAC lifecycle, a detailed site assessment and heat-load study, right-sized system selection, clean installation and commissioning, followed by testing, balancing and a structured maintenance handover. The outcome is dependable comfort with efficient, predictable operation.

The brief

CoreEL Technologies approached us with a facility that combines electronics manufacturing lines, dedicated R&D and testing labs, and administrative office space under one roof. Each zone carries a distinct thermal and air-quality profile, production areas generate concentrated equipment heat, while R&D and testing spaces demand stable, tightly controlled temperature and humidity to protect sensitive components and instrumentation. The brief called for a system that could hold precise, consistent conditions in the critical zones without over-cooling the wider facility, while keeping running costs and maintenance overheads predictable over the long term. Energy efficiency was an explicit priority, given how much of the facility runs cooling loads continuously across shifts. Reliability mattered just as much as precision, since any unplanned interruption in the R&D zones could affect ongoing tests and development timelines.

Site & operational constraints

The facility was operational through much of the engagement, so the design had to account for continuous production shifts, live equipment racks and staggered lab schedules. Heat loads varied significantly from one zone to the next, and several areas carried legacy ducting and electrical routing that limited where new equipment could sit. Any prolonged shutdown of cooling to the R&D or precision areas risked affecting ongoing tests, so sequencing and access planning were as important as the mechanical design itself. Coordinating installation windows with the client's shift patterns and equipment vendors required close, ongoing communication throughout the project.

Our engineered solution

We proposed a VRF-led architecture layered with dedicated precision cooling for the most sensitive zones, giving the client independent temperature control across production, lab and office areas rather than a single shared setpoint. Indoor units and air distribution were positioned to even out temperature gradients across the production floor, while the precision-cooling zones were designed around tighter tolerance bands and closer humidity control. Filtration and air-change planning were tuned to the electronics environment to reduce airborne particulate risk around sensitive assembly and testing work, and the overall layout was built with headroom for future capacity as the facility's equipment load evolves. Controls were configured so the facility team could monitor and adjust individual zones on their own, giving them day-to-day flexibility without needing to call us in for routine changes.

Right-sized VRF hardware

Equipment was selected zone by zone rather than off a single spec sheet, matching outdoor capacity and indoor unit types to the actual load and tolerance band each area needed, from the open production floor to the precision-controlled labs.

Execution & commissioning

Installation was phased zone by zone in coordination with CoreEL's facility and production teams, so critical operations were never left without conditioning for long. Refrigerant piping, controls wiring and ductwork were routed to work around existing services rather than disrupt them. Once installed, each zone went through individual testing and balancing before being signed off, followed by a full-facility performance check to confirm that the zones worked correctly together as one coordinated system. Documentation covering equipment specifications, zone mapping and control settings was handed over alongside the physical installation, giving the client's team a clear reference point for future servicing.

The outcome

CoreEL now runs its manufacturing, R&D and office spaces on a system engineered for their specific thermal profile rather than a generic layout. Sensitive equipment and testing areas hold stable conditions independent of production floor loads, and the facility team has clear zone-wise control for day-to-day adjustments. With commissioning documentation and a structured AMC in place, the system is positioned for consistent, low-disruption performance as CoreEL's operations continue to grow. As the company adds equipment or expands specific zones, the underlying VRF architecture allows incremental capacity to be added without a full system redesign.

Outcome. Sensitive R&D and testing zones now hold stable conditions independent of production floor loads, with clear zone-wise control for CoreEL's facility team.
