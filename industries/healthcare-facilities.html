Why hospital HVAC is a different discipline

In most buildings, HVAC exists for comfort. In a hospital, it's part of clinical infrastructure. Operation theatres, ICUs, isolation wards and general wards each carry different requirements for air changes, pressure relationships and filtration, and getting these wrong doesn't just affect comfort, it affects infection control and patient outcomes. We treat healthcare HVAC as an engineering discipline in its own right, working closely with facility and infection-control teams rather than applying a standard commercial design.

Every department is assessed separately. An OT needs controlled positive pressure and a defined number of air changes per hour; an isolation room may need the opposite, negative pressure to contain airborne contaminants. Wards and OPDs have their own occupancy and airflow patterns. Our design process maps these requirements room by room before any equipment is selected, and we document these parameters clearly so they can be verified during commissioning and revisited at every subsequent AMC visit.

Air changes, filtration and infection control

Filtration and air-change rates are specified to match each area's clinical use, HEPA filtration and higher air-change rates in OTs and critical care areas, standard filtration in general wards and administrative zones. We size ductwork and diffuser placement to avoid dead zones where air doesn't circulate properly, and we pay close attention to pressure cascades between adjacent spaces, so air moves in the intended direction between corridors, anterooms and clinical areas. Duct and diffuser layouts are also planned for ease of cleaning and periodic validation, since infection-control audits routinely check these areas alongside clinical protocols.

Temperature and humidity control across departments

Bengaluru's humidity swings through the year make humidity control as important as temperature in a healthcare setting, excess moisture affects everything from patient comfort in wards to the reliability of sensitive equipment in labs and pharmacies. We design cooling and dehumidification together rather than relying on temperature setpoints alone, particularly in OTs and ICUs where both parameters are typically specified within tight ranges.

Different departments run on different schedules. OPDs follow daytime patterns, while ICUs, OTs and emergency areas need consistent conditions around the clock, we design zoning and controls so each department can be managed independently without one area's schedule compromising another's.

Uptime and redundancy, built in

A hospital cannot simply switch off the air conditioning while a unit is repaired. For critical areas, OTs, ICUs, NICUs, we design with standby capacity and, where the facility requires it, redundant units that can take over without interrupting conditions in the space. Our AMC contracts for healthcare clients prioritise rapid response and preventive checks precisely because unplanned downtime in these areas is not an option. Where budgets allow, we also recommend standby chillers or condensing units for the facility's most critical zones, so a single equipment fault never becomes a clinical risk.

From assessment to long-term AMC support

Our process for healthcare facilities begins with a detailed site and department-wise assessment, working alongside hospital administration and, where needed, biomedical or infection-control consultants. Design, installation and commissioning follow, with validation of air changes and pressure relationships before handover. Ongoing AMC includes scheduled filter and duct checks, calibration verification and priority breakdown response, keeping OTs, ICUs and wards running to the standards patient care demands.
