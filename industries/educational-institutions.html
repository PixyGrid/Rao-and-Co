Plan Your Campus HVAC

Every space on campus cools differently

A school or college campus is really several different buildings under one roof. A classroom with thirty students generates a very different heat and CO2 load than a science lab with equipment running, or an auditorium that sits empty most days and then fills to capacity for an exam or event. Applying one system design across all of these rarely works, classrooms end up too warm during peak teaching hours, while labs and auditoriums are either overcooled between uses or under-ventilated exactly when it matters.

We start by understanding how each space is actually used, student strength per room, class timings, lab equipment heat loads, and event frequency for auditoriums and seminar halls, before sizing anything. This is what keeps classrooms comfortable through a full teaching day without oversizing equipment that then sits idle for the rest of it, and it's the same reasoning we apply when a campus is phased or expanded over several years.

Ventilation and indoor air quality for students

Classrooms pack more people into less area than almost any other commercial space, and children and young adults are more sensitive to poor air quality, it shows up quickly as drowsiness and lost concentration in the later periods of the day. We treat fresh-air ventilation as a core design requirement, not an add-on, sizing air changes to occupancy rather than floor area alone, and we account for the extra load that shared spaces such as libraries and computer labs place on ventilation during exam season.

Quiet operation and holiday-aware scheduling

Noise is a genuine design constraint in a classroom in a way it rarely is in an office. Compressor vibration or a noisy indoor unit competes directly with a teacher's voice, so we select and mount equipment specifically to keep operating noise low near desks and blackboards, and position units to avoid direct draughts on students seated closest to them.

Academic calendars shape how we programme these systems. Term breaks, exam schedules and staggered class timings mean large stretches of the year when only parts of a campus are in use, so we set up zone-wise, time-based scheduling, a meaningful saving over a full academic year.

Working within institutional budgets

Educational institutions typically plan HVAC spend around annual budgets and long equipment life, not just upfront capital cost. We factor total cost of ownership into our recommendations, energy-efficient VRF where campus size justifies it, simpler ducted or split systems where it doesn't, and we're upfront when a lower-cost option is genuinely adequate for a space, rather than always proposing the larger system. AMC contracts are structured around the academic year, with servicing scheduled during vacations wherever possible so classrooms aren't disrupted during term.

Why institutions choose Rao & Co

Trustees, principals and facility heads work with us because we design for the way a campus actually runs, mixed-use spaces, seasonal occupancy and tight budgets, rather than a generic commercial template. From the first site assessment through installation, commissioning and ongoing AMC, our engineers stay involved so classrooms, labs and halls remain comfortable and well-ventilated across every term.
