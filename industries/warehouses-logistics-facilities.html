The scale problem: cooling and ventilating very large volumes

Warehouses and logistics facilities present a scale problem that most HVAC systems aren't built for. High roofs mean heat stratifies, with hot air collecting well above the working zone while the floor level, where people and goods actually are, stays cooler but poorly ventilated. Large floor plates mean air has to move a long way from any single source before it reaches every corner, and in Bengaluru's warm, humid months, stagnant air pockets in the upper reaches of a warehouse can push ambient temperatures at roof level uncomfortably high even when the working floor feels manageable.

Standard air conditioning, designed for enclosed rooms, simply isn't the right tool for a 10-metre-high shed with a footprint measured in thousands of square metres.

When storage conditions become a specification

The goods stored also matter. Some inventory tolerates warehouse conditions without issue; other stock, electronics, pharmaceuticals, certain packaged goods, needs a defined temperature or humidity band maintained consistently, which turns the problem from general comfort into a controlled-environment specification.

How we approach ventilation and cooling at warehouse scale

We start by separating the two problems warehouses usually face: bulk air movement to prevent heat build-up and stagnation, and, where needed, targeted temperature control for specific zones or stored goods. For general working comfort, large-volume ventilation, moving air rather than chilling all of it, is often the more practical and energy-sensible solution across a big shed. Where specific zones or products need tighter control, we design localised cooling rather than trying to condition the entire volume to a uniform standard, which would be both impractical and expensive at that scale.

Equipment we typically deploy in warehouse and logistics settings

High-volume, low-speed fans and roof-mounted extraction handle bulk air movement and heat stratification in the main working volume, drawing hot air out at height and encouraging fresh air circulation at floor level. For zones needing closer temperature control, packing areas, cold-sensitive storage, or office and control-room sections within a larger facility, we deploy ducted or VRF systems that can be zoned independently of the main shed. Loading dock areas, which see constant exposure to outside air, are treated as their own zone rather than expected to hold the same conditions as sealed storage.

Away from the main shed, smaller enclosed rooms still need conventional comfort cooling, sized and zoned on their own terms rather than tied to the warehouse's bulk ventilation.

Worker comfort, goods protection and energy efficiency at scale

Warehouse HVAC has to serve two different needs at once, protecting the goods in storage and keeping working conditions safe and tolerable for staff moving through the facility for full shifts. Poor ventilation doesn't just make a warehouse uncomfortable; it can also affect goods sensitive to heat and humidity, and it raises real workplace safety concerns during Bengaluru's warmer months. Because the volumes involved are so large, energy efficiency is not optional, moving or conditioning air across a big facility inefficiently shows up directly and significantly on the electricity bill, so system selection and control strategy are chosen with that scale in mind from the outset.

Key point. Poor ventilation doesn't just make a warehouse uncomfortable, it can affect goods sensitive to heat and humidity and raise real workplace safety concerns during Bengaluru's warmer months.

Why warehouse and logistics operators choose Rao & Co

Operators come to us because we don't propose a single blanket solution for a building that clearly has different zones with different needs, a loading dock, a bulk storage area and a packing office all require different approaches, and we design accordingly. Our process begins with a full site assessment covering roof height, layout and stored goods, moves through a clear proposal and clean installation, and continues with AMC support that accounts for the practical realities of servicing equipment mounted at height or spread across a large footprint.
